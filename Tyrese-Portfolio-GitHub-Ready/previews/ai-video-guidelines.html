AI-Assisted Video Creation for Learning &
Communications
Purpose:
To provide a repeatable workflow for converting a project description into a professional AI-
assisted learning or communications video.

Audience:
Creative Learning, Research & Communications Interns and other team members involved
in AI-assisted video production.

Core principle:
AI is a production co-creator, not an end-to-end replacement for human creative,
instructional, editorial, and brand judgment. The human creator owns the message,
narrative, visual direction, quality control, and final approval.




1. The End-to-End Workflow
The standard workflow is:



 Project description → Project summary → Script → Voiceover → Concepts/scenes
 → AI images → Image-to-video → Video assembly → Voiceover → Music → Quality
 control → Final export


The process should be modular rather than relying on one AI system to generate an entire
finished video. It is recommended to use AI for individual audiovisual assets while retaining
human control over sequencing, pacing, storytelling, and final review.

2. Stage 1 — Understand the Project
Before opening an AI tool, understand what the video is supposed to communicate.

Identify:

    ●   What is the project?
    ●   Who is the audience?
    ●   What should the audience learn, understand, or feel?
    ●   What are the key messages?
    ●   What evidence, facts or source material must be represented accurately?
    ●   What is the intended duration?
    ●   Where will the video be used?
    ●   Are there brand, legal, privacy, or confidentiality requirements?

Do not begin image generation before the narrative purpose is clear.

Human responsibility
The creator must provide the creative and instructional direction. AI should help accelerate
production rather than determine the educational message.

Learning designers retain professional identity through pedagogical intent, human oversight,
and curricular alignment.

3. Stage 2 — Create the Project Summary
Turn the project description into a concise summary.

The summary should answer:

   1.   What is the project?
   2.   Why does it matter?
   3.   What problem or opportunity does it address?
   4.   What is being done?
   5.   What should the viewer take away?

Keep the summary factual and avoid introducing information that is not supported by the
project material. This summary becomes the foundation for the script.




4. Stage 3 — Convert the Summary into a
Script
Use the project summary and source material to create the narration script.

The script should:

   ●    Have a clear beginning, middle, and end.
   ●    Follow a logical narrative.
   ●    Explain rather than merely describe.
   ●    Match the intended audience.
   ●    Use accessible language.
   ●    Contain only claims that can be supported.
   ●    Provide enough visual opportunities for the animation.
   ●    Avoid ideas that cannot reasonably be represented visually.

Script review gate
Before moving to image generation, ask:

   ●    Does every sentence contribute to the story?
   ●    Is the information accurate?
   ●    Is the pacing appropriate?
   ●    Can each major idea be represented visually?
   ●    Are there unnecessary details?
   ●    Is the script suitable for the intended video length?

Only proceed once the script has been reviewed and approved.




5. Stage 4 — Produce the Voiceover
Page 4 / 19
Once the script is approved, send the final script through the company's voiceover process.
Janus does all the voice overs. The voiceover becomes the timing anchor for the video.
Record or generate the voiceover before finalising the video sequence where possible,
because scene length should correspond to what is being said.




6. Stage 5 — Break the Script into Visual
Concepts
Divide the script into logical scenes or concepts. A concept should represent one clear visual
idea.

For example:

Narration:
"The interns use digital systems to analyse manufacturing data."

Visual concept:
Young interns working around a manufacturing workstation while interacting with cyan digital
data visualisations.

Do not simply divide the script according to punctuation. Divide it according to what needs to
be shown.

7. Stage 6 — Establish the Master Visual
Style
Before generating images, establish one master visual style.

The style should follow the following guidelines:

MASTER VISUAL STYLE
Handcrafted Pencil-and-Ink Illustrative Animation

The visual identity combines:

   ●   Precise black ink linework.
   ●   Rich coloured-pencil shading.
   ●   Visible hand-rendered texture.
   ●   A sophisticated technical-manual aesthetic.
   ●   Blue manufacturing environments.
   ●   Neon cyan digital/VR interfaces.
   ●   Young, diverse, stylised professional characters.
   ●   Specialised, tailored manufacturing attire.

This style is non-negotiable.

Environment
Maintain a recognisable blue manufacturing environment throughout:

   ●   Manufacturing machinery.
   ●   Workstations.
   ●   Digital equipment.
   ●   Industrial structures.
   ●   Technical work areas.
   ●   Blue-grey surfaces.
   ●   Advanced workplace technology.

Visual accent
Use neon cyan for:

   ●   Data visualisations.
   ●   Digital interfaces.
   ●   Network maps.
   ●   Technical schematics.
   ●   Graphs.
   ●   Sensors.
   ●   VR interfaces.
   ●   Digital connections.
   ●   Technical overlays.

8. Character Consistency
Whenever possible, establish a small recurring cast.

Once a character appears, maintain:

    ●   Same appearance.
    ●   Same hairstyle.
    ●   Same clothing.
    ●   Same visual features.
    ●   Same general age.
    ●   Same design language.

Characters should be:

    ●   Young adults.
    ●   Visually diverse.
    ●   Diverse in ethnicity and gender.
    ●   Professional.
    ●   Expressive but stylised.

Clothing
Avoid generic work uniforms.

Use designed, specialised manufacturing attire such as:

    ●   Tailored aprons.
    ●   Structured waistcoats.
    ●   Technical suits.
    ●   Specialised blue-grey workwear.
    ●   Contemporary manufacturing garments.




9. What NOT to Generate
The visual style must not drift into:

    ●   Photorealism.
    ●   Live action.
    ●   Realistic human faces.
    ●   Realistic hands.
    ●   Realistic anatomy.
    ●   3D CGI characters.
    ●   Plastic-looking CGI.
    ●   Generic stock-video people.
    ●   Anime.
    ●   Cartoon comedy.
    ●   Corporate stock footage.
Page 7 / 19
    ●    Documentary footage.
    ●    Generic futuristic sci-fi.

The desired effect is:

"A technical illustration coming to life."

not:

"A realistic film generated by AI."




10. On-Screen Text and Logos
During AI image/video generation
Do not ask the AI generator to create readable text.

Avoid:

    ●    Titles.
    ●    Captions.
    ●    Subtitles.
    ●    Labels.
    ●    Headings.
    ●    Written instructions.
    ●    Floating text.
    ●    Text overlays.
    ●    Logos.
    ●    Watermarks.
    ●    Corporate marks.

Instead, represent information with:

    ●    Abstract scribbles.
    ●    Lines.
    ●    Graphs.
    ●    Symbols.
    ●    Shapes.
    ●    Data-like marks.
    ●    Cyan interface patterns.

Any form of text should be manually inserted into the video clip. This is to avoid typography
issues produced by the AI video generating platform.

If text or logos are required
Add them manually in post-production.

This is particularly important for:

    ●    Correct spelling.
Page 8 / 19
   ●   Company logos.
   ●   Brand marks.
   ●   Legal text.
   ●   Key claims.
   ●   Packaging text.
   ●   Titles and labels.

AI-generated text and logos are incorrect; it is recommended adding brand-critical elements
and any text manually during the final editing/brand pass.

For text, the recommended workflow is:

AI scene → blank/squiggle area → correct text added in CapCut/editor → human
QC




11. Generate the Images
For each scene, generate an image that visually communicates the narration.

Image-generation rules
Every image should:

   1. Match the script.
   2. Follow the master visual style.
   3. Use the same visual identity as the other scenes.
   4. Maintain character consistency.
   5. Maintain the blue manufacturing environment.
   6. Use neon cyan appropriately.
   7. Avoid readable text.
   8. Avoid logos.
   9. Avoid photorealism.
   10. Clearly communicate the intended concept.

Golden rule
Do not generate an attractive image first and then try to make the script fit it.

Instead:

Script → concept → image

The narration determines the action and composition.




12. Image Quality Check
Page 9 / 19
Before using an image as a video source, inspect it.

Check:
Style

   ●    Does it look illustrated?
   ●    Is the pencil-and-ink style obvious?
   ●    Is the coloured-pencil texture present?

Environment

   ●    Does the manufacturing environment look consistent?

Characters

   ●    Are characters stylised rather than photorealistic?
   ●    Do recurring characters remain consistent?

Story

   ●    Does the image actually show what the narration is saying?

AI defects

   ●    Are hands, faces, machinery and objects coherent?
   ●    Are there distorted objects?
   ●    Are there impossible physical relationships?
   ●    Is anything unintentionally duplicated?

Text

   ●    Is there any unwanted readable text?

Brand

   ●    Are there unwanted logos or brand marks?

Do not move poor-quality images into video generation simply because they are "good
enough."




13. Create Video-Generation Prompts
Once the image is approved, use ChatGPT to create:

   1. A master video-generation prompt.
   2. An individual prompt for each scene.

The video prompt should instruct the AI to animate the supplied image rather than redesign
it.
Page 10 / 19
Required principles
The generated animation must:

   ●    Use the exact supplied imagery.
   ●    Preserve the existing visual style.
   ●    Preserve character appearance.
   ●    Preserve the environment.
   ●    Match the narration.
   ●    Use purposeful motion.
   ●    Avoid unnecessary camera movement.
   ●    Contain no sound.

Recommendation: Generate approximately 8–10 second clips, with the number of clips
determined by the length of each script scene. For example, the image for one scene can be
converted into 1 or more video clips, depending on the length of that scene.




14. Prompt for Purposeful Motion
Avoid vague prompts such as:

       "Make this image move."

Instead, specify:

   ●    Who/what moves.
   ●    What the movement is.
   ●    Direction of movement.
   ●    What happens first.
   ●    What happens next.
   ●    Camera behaviour.
   ●    Environmental movement.
   ●    How the scene ends.

Example structure
Subject + Action + Direction + Environment + Camera + Timing + Constraints

For example:

       Animate the illustrated intern as they reach toward the floating cyan data
       interface and examine the changing visual data. The interface responds with
       controlled cyan data flows while the manufacturing machinery moves subtly in
       the background. Maintain the exact Pencil-and-Ink Illustrative style, character
       design and blue manufacturing environment. Use a restrained camera
       movement. No text, logos or sound.

15. Avoid Repetitive AI Motion
AI video generators can produce:

   ●   Repeated gestures.
   ●   Cyclical arm movements.
   ●   Walking loops.
   ●   Frozen movement.
   ●   Jitter.
   ●   Unnatural trajectories.
   ●   Objects changing shape between frames.

Explicitly specify movement and trajectory rather than relying on unconstrained text prompts.

Practical prompt techniques
Specify:

   ●   A clear start position.
   ●   A clear end position.
   ●   A directional action.
   ●   A specific spatial target.
   ●   One primary movement.
   ●   Subtle secondary movement.

For example:

Weak:
"Person interacts with the computer."

Better:
"The intern slowly raises their right hand toward the floating cyan interface, touches the
central data node, and moves their hand from left to right as the connected data pathways
illuminate."




16. Generate the Video Clips
Use the approved image and its scene-specific prompt in the selected video-generation
platform, such as:

   ●   Google Flow.
   ●   Google Vids.
   ●   Another approved GenAI video platform.

Generate the clip without audio.

Review every generated clip
Do not automatically accept the first generation.
Page 12 / 19
Check:

   ●     Does the movement make physical sense?
   ●     Does the character remain consistent?
   ●     Does the image remain visually faithful?
   ●     Does the animation match the narration?
   ●     Does anything warp?
   ●     Does anything disappear?
   ●     Does the background change?
   ●     Is there unwanted text?
   ●     Is there an unwanted logo?
   ●     Does the movement repeat unnaturally?
   ●     Is the camera movement appropriate?

If not, revise the prompt and regenerate. This human-in-the-loop process is essential
because AI video can produce structural warping, unnatural actions and semantic drift. Due
to credit limitations, ensure that it is prompted correctly to avoid regeneration.




17. Assemble the Video
Once the clips have passed visual quality control, move them into CapCut or Google Vids.
Place the clips in the order of the script.

Then:

   1.    Align each visual scene with its corresponding narration.
   2.    Trim clips to match the voiceover.
   3.    Extend or shorten visual sections where necessary.
   4.    Add transitions only where they support the story.
   5.    Add required text manually.
   6.    Add approved logos manually where required.
   7.    Check visual continuity.

The video should feel like one coherent story, not a collection of unrelated AI clips.




18. Add the Voiceover
Import the approved voiceover.

Synchronise the narration with the scenes.

Check:
   ●     Does the visual change when the narration changes concept?
   ●     Is important information being shown while it is spoken?
   ●     Are scenes too short or too long?
   ●     Are there awkward gaps?
Page 13 / 19
   ●   Does the pacing feel natural?
   ●   Does the visual emphasis match the verbal emphasis?

Remember:

The voiceover is the narrative spine. The visuals should support it rather than compete
with it.




19. Add Background Music
Once the voiceover and visuals are locked, add appropriate background music.

Pixabay can be used as a source for background music, however other sources/platforms
can be. Ensure that the background music is free to use and not licensed.

Music should:

   ●   Support the tone.
   ●   Sit underneath the voiceover.
   ●   Not overpower speech.
   ●   Not distract from the learning message.
   ●   Be appropriate for the intended audience and context.
   ●   Be checked for licensing/usage requirements according to company policy.




20. Final Quality-Control Gate
No video should be released without human review. The final reviewer should assess five
areas:

A. Content
   ●   Is the information accurate?
   ●   Does the video communicate the intended message?
   ●   Are claims supported?
   ●   Is anything misleading?

B. Instructional quality
   ●   Does the visual support the narration?
   ●   Is the pacing appropriate?
   ●   Is the story easy to follow?
   ●   Does the video support the learning objective?

C. Visual quality
   ●   Is the visual style consistent?
Page 14 / 19
   ●    Are characters consistent?
   ●    Are there distortions?
   ●    Are there repetitive movements?
   ●    Is the animation smooth?
   ●    Are the visuals directly relevant?

D. Brand quality
   ●    Are logos correct?
   ●    Is manually added text correct?
   ●    Are colours and brand elements approved?
   ●    Are there accidental AI-generated logos or brand marks?

E. Technical quality
   ●    Is the voiceover clear?
   ●    Is the music balanced?
   ●    Is there unwanted sound?
   ●    Are transitions clean?
   ●    Is the final resolution correct?
   ●    Does the exported file play correctly?

Human review remains essential because AI-generated video can be visually convincing
while still being scientifically, physically, narratively or contextually incorrect.




21. Final Export and File Management
Maintain organised project files so that another intern can continue the work.

Keep:

   ●    Final script.
   ●    Approved voiceover.
   ●    Scene table.
   ●    Final image for each scene.
   ●    Video-generation prompts.
   ●    Final video clips.
   ●    Editing project.
   ●    Music source/licensing information where applicable.
   ●    Final exported video.

This makes the workflow transferable to the next intern rather than leaving the process
dependent on one person's memory.

23. The "No AI Slop" Rule
AI slop is low-quality, high-volume synthetic content produced with minimal human
discretion. The antidote is not simply "better prompts." It is a structured production
process with human judgement at every important stage.

The workflow should therefore always follow:

Intent → Script → Storyboard/Concept → Image → Animation → Human QC → Edit
→ Human QC → Final

Avoid:

One prompt → Generate entire video → Publish

The guidelines emphasise structured creative intent, multimodal quality checking and
iterative human-in-the-loop workflows as key methods for reducing AI-generated defects.

26. The Golden Rules for Future Interns
If you remember nothing else, remember these:

1. Start with the story, not the AI tool.

2. The narration determines the visuals.

3. Generate images first, then animate them.

4. Keep one visual identity from beginning to end.

5. Use recurring characters consistently.

6. Never rely on AI to generate final readable text or brand-critical logos.

7. Keep generated video silent; add voiceover and music during editing.

8. Tell the AI exactly what should move and where it should move.

9. Regenerate bad clips—do not "hope nobody notices."

10. AI creates; humans curate, correct and approve.

11. Every scene must earn its place by explaining something from the narration.

12. The final video should look intentionally designed, not mass-generated.




Final Principle
The objective of this workflow is not simply to make videos faster.

It is to use AI to accelerate repetitive production work while preserving human ownership
of instructional intent, storytelling, visual consistency, accuracy, brand identity and
final quality.

AI should make the intern's workflow faster. It should not make the thinking less rigorous.
